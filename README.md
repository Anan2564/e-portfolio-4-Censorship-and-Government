Link: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10383441.2024.2405760

Summary of the artefact: 

Smith, Nolan, and Gaffey analyze the regulation of online safety in
Australia, focusing on the Federal Court case that stemmed from the
eSafety Commissioner demanding the removal of videos depicting the
church stabbing in Wakeley, 2024. They identify three avenues through
which the government can take action: removal notices based on the
Online Safety Act 2021, abhorrent violent material legislation, and
hate symbol offenses — pointing out the limited scope of one country
regulating a global network (Smith, Nolan & Gaffey 2024, p. XX).

Justification on why I chose the artefact:

The reason I chose this artefact is that it has direct relation to the workshop discussion regarding government censorship and the problem of controlling content on an international network. The artefact provides an example of the dilemma between securing citizens from exposure to harmful information and the principle of freedom of speech. At the same time, it proves that even if governments possess the right to control online platforms, it might be impossible due to the international nature of those platforms.

Link: https://freedomhouse.org/report/freedom-net/2025/uncertain-future-global-internet

Summary of the artefact: 

Freedom on the Net 2025 evaluates internet freedom in 72 nations from June
2024 to May 2025. There has been the fifteenth straight year of an annual
decline in internet freedom globally, with conditions getting worse in
27 nations and better in 17. Myanmar and China rank the lowest while
Iceland ranks the highest. More than half of the 18 countries that are
classified as "free" have lost points, and more people have been arrested
for their online activities than ever before (Freedom House 2025).

Justification on why I chose the artefact:

I have selected this particular artefact as it gives a global view of Internet censorship, thereby showing that there are wide differences in how governments control information on the Internet. This artefact has a strong link with the discussion on Internet freedom in the global context in the workshop session. This report made me realize the effects of Internet censorship on freedom of speech when the government uses Internet censorship to control or silence any criticism. As an ICT student, I believe it is essential to learn about such issues.

Artefact 3 — ABC News, High Court challenge to the under-16s social media ban

Summary of the artefact:

On 26 November 2025, the Digital Freedom Project, alongside two 15 year olds, initiated a constitutional action against the Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024. The High Court determined to hear the matter on 4 December and gave it the name of S163/2025. The applicants claim that the age restrictions impose an improper burden on the implied freedom of political communication. However, the ban commenced on 10 December 2025 (ABC News 2025).

Justification on why I chose the artefact:

This artefact was selected by me since it is one of the contemporary examples from Australia where the use of social media is regulated by the government. It presents the ethical dilemma of whether the protection of teenagers on the Internet would be enough justification for restricting their access to social media. It also relates to the issue discussed during the workshop when we asked ourselves under what circumstances the protection of society on the Internet turns into the restriction of freedom.
