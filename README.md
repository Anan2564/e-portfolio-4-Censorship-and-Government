Summary of the artefact:

Smith, Nolan, and Gaffey analyse the regulation of online safety in Australia, focusing on the Federal Court case that stemmed from the eSafety Commissioner demanding the removal of videos of the Wakeley church stabbing, 2024. They identify three avenues for government action: removal notices based on the Online Safety Act 2021, abhorrent violent material legislation, and hate symbol offences — pointing out the limited scope of one country regulating a global network (Smith, Nolan & Gaffey 2024).

Type: Research report  

Link: https://freedomhouse.org/report/freedom-net/2025/uncertain-future-global-internet

Summary of the artefact:

Freedom on the Net 2025 evaluates internet freedom in 72 nations from June 2024 to May 2025. It is the fifteenth straight year of decline in internet freedom globally, with conditions getting worse in 27 nations and better in 17. Myanmar and China rank the lowest while Iceland ranks the highest. Half of the 18 countries classified as free have lost points, and more people have been arrested for their online activities than ever before (Freedom House 2025).

Justification on why I chose the artefact:

I selected this artefact as it gives a global view of Internet censorship, showing wide differences in how governments control information on the Internet. This artefact links strongly with the workshop discussion on Internet freedom in the global context. This report made me realise the effects of Internet censorship on freedom of speech when the government uses it to control or silence criticism.

In addition, the Digital Freedom Project, along with two 15-year-olds, launched a constitutional challenge against the Online Safety Amendment (Social Media Minimum Age) Act 2024 on 26 November 2025. The High Court chose to consider the case on 4 December 2025 as S163/2025. The parties involved in the case believe that the minimum age requirement creates an undue burden on the implied freedom of political communication.
